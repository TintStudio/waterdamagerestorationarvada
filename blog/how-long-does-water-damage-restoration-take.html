How Long Does Water Damage Restoration Take?

When your home suffers water damage, the most pressing question is usually, "When will things get back to normal?" Understanding the timeline helps manage expectations during a stressful period.

1. Factors That Affect Restoration Timeline

There is no one-size-fits-all answer to how long restoration takes, as every water damage event is unique. The timeline heavily depends on the volume of water, how long the water was sitting before mitigation started, and the types of materials affected (e.g., drywall absorbs water differently than hardwood floors).

Additionally, the presence of hazardous materials like asbestos in older Arvada homes, or the development of mold, can significantly extend the timeline. Generally, the process is divided into mitigation (drying and cleanup) and reconstruction (putting things back together).

2. Water Extraction: Usually 1-2 Days

The immediate priority is removing standing water. If a team from a professional water damage restoration company arrives quickly, physical water extraction usually takes one to two days. Technicians use powerful truck-mounted vacuums and submersible pumps to remove thousands of gallons of water quickly.

During this phase, unsalvageable materials like ruined carpet padding, heavily saturated drywall, and ruined personal belongings are removed from the property. Getting the bulk of the water out fast is critical to setting up the structural drying phase for success.

3. Structural Drying: Typically 3-5 Days

Even after standing water is gone, building materials remain saturated. The structural drying phase typically takes between 3 to 5 days, though dense materials like hardwood flooring or concrete can take a week or longer. Professionals will set up an array of high-velocity air movers and industrial LGR (Low Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifiers.

Technicians will return daily to monitor moisture levels using specialized meters and thermal imaging cameras, adjusting the equipment as needed. It is vital that homeowners do not turn off this equipment, as continuous operation is required to prevent mold growth and ensure deep drying.

4. Mold Prevention and Treatment: 1-3 Days If Needed

If water sat for more than 48 hours before mitigation began, mold is likely to develop. If mold remediation is required, it adds steps to the timeline. The area must be contained, the mold safely removed, and antimicrobial treatments applied to prevent return.

Depending on the severity of the contamination, this can add anywhere from 1 to 3 days (or more for severe cases) to the mitigation phase before reconstruction can safely begin. Following industry standard S520 guidelines is crucial during this phase to ensure the indoor air quality is restored.

5. Repairs and Reconstruction: 1-2 Weeks or More

Once the property is certified dry and safe, reconstruction begins. This phase is highly variable. If only minor drywall patching and painting are required, it might take a few days. However, if entire rooms need new flooring, baseboards, cabinets, and extensive drywall replacement, reconstruction can take 1 to 2 weeks or longer.

The timeline for this phase is also subject to contractor availability, insurance approval delays, and supply chain issues for specific materials like custom cabinetry or specific flooring matches.

6. Category of Water Damage Matters

The source of the water—known as its Category—impacts the timeline. Category 1 (Clean Water) from a burst supply line is the fastest to clean up. Category 2 (Gray Water), such as overflow from a washing machine, contains contaminants and requires more aggressive cleaning protocols.

Category 3 (Black Water), which includes raw sewage backups or natural flood damage, is highly hazardous. Cleanup requires extensive demolition of porous materials, rigorous sanitization, and strict safety protocols, significantly extending the overall timeline.

7. Size and Severity of the Damage

Naturally, a small leak affecting a single bathroom will be resolved much faster than a multi-story flood caused by a broken pipe on the top floor that cascaded down through the entire house. The sheer volume of square footage affected dictates how much equipment is needed and how much demolition must occur.

In large commercial losses, or homes with extensive square footage affected, project managers must carefully sequence the work, which can stretch the mitigation phase by several days.

8. How Weather and Humidity Affect Drying in Colorado

Colorado's arid climate can actually be an advantage during the structural drying phase. The naturally low humidity in Arvada and the Denver metro area often helps accelerate the evaporation process when combined with professional air movers and dehumidification.

However, during wet spring months or if dealing with a damp, poorly ventilated crawlspace, the external environment can fight against the drying equipment. Professionals use psychrometry (the science of drying) to create the optimal indoor environment regardless of the weather outside.

9. What You Can Do to Help the Process

Homeowners can facilitate a faster restoration process by acting quickly. Call a mitigation company immediately upon discovering the damage. Do not delay waiting for an insurance adjuster, as most policies require you to mitigate further damage promptly.

During the drying phase, leave the equipment running 24/7. Turning off fans or dehumidifiers at night because they are noisy will stall the drying process and give mold a chance to take hold, ultimately extending the project timeline and increasing costs.

10. When Restoration Takes Longer Than Expected

Delays can happen. The most common bottlenecks involve waiting for insurance approvals for the reconstruction phase, or discovering hazardous materials like asbestos in the drywall joint compound (common in pre-1980s Arvada homes), which requires specialized abatement before normal demolition can proceed.

Maintaining open communication with your restoration project manager and your insurance adjuster is the best way to keep the project moving forward as smoothly as possible.
